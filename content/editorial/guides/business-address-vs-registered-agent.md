## The short answer

A business address and a registered agent are different roles. A virtual-office provider may sell one, both, or neither for a particular location. Never assume that an address product includes registered-agent service.

For a Florida LLC, the Florida Department of State says the registered agent is the individual or legal entity that accepts service of process for the entity and must have a physical street address in Florida, not a P.O. Box. The state separately requests a principal-office street address and may request a distinct mailing address.

## What to verify before filing

Confirm the entity type and filing instructions with the Florida Department of State. Confirm that the exact provider or named agent has agreed to serve as registered agent. Confirm every address field the filing form requires. If a provider calls an address “business” or “virtual,” that alone does not answer the registered-agent question.

Opus’s terms expressly say its virtual-office address is not a registered-office address unless registered-agent services are explicitly included. Alliance’s product guide says to ask for recommendations of registered-agent services, which is not the same as including one.
