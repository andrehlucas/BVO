## The short answer

A business address is usually the narrower product: an address for business materials and often mail receipt. A virtual office may add phone handling, reception, and workspace access. The dividing line is the specific plan, not the label alone.

Regus’s Miami page makes the distinction directly. It describes its Business Address as an address where mail can be collected or forwarding arranged, while its Virtual Office adds local phone and call-handling services. Its Virtual Office Plus adds five days of workspace access each month.

## Compare the actual inclusions

A productive comparison asks: Is mail receipt included? Is forwarding extra? Is a local phone number included? Is a live receptionist included, optional, or merely available? Does workspace mean an included allowance or pay-per-use booking?

Do not use a business-address price to represent the cost of phone answering or a meeting room. Do not use an advertised virtual-office price to assume mail scanning or forwarding is included.

## Pick the relevant track

Choose **Address & mail** when mail receipt and a business address are the essential confirmed features. Choose **Receptionist & phone** only when live answer and a phone or call-forwarding method are included. Choose **Full virtual office** only when both sides are shown as one plan or as explicitly compatible purchasable components.
