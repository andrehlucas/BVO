## The quick verdict

Opus is built around a simple proposition: buy a business address and live receptionist together for a widely advertised $99 monthly rate. That combination can be attractive when both services solve a real problem for your business. It is less compelling when you need only an address, need a precisely defined call allowance, or expect the same workspace access at every location.

The strongest reason to consider Opus is not the word “all-inclusive.” It is the breadth of the core bundle. The strongest reason to slow down is that the marketing page and the legal terms do not describe usage limits in exactly the same way. Confirm the selected location, final checkout price, setup charge, call treatment and mail instructions before subscribing.

## Where Opus fits in the national market

Opus competes as a bundled virtual-office provider rather than an address-only marketplace. The provider says it offers more than 650 locations across the United States and Canada. Best Virtual Offices does not treat that number as independently verified; it is Opus’s description of its own network as of this review.

Its national offer is easiest to understand as two starting points:

- **Address-focused service:** Opus publishes a separate corporate mailing address product at selected locations. The displayed Florida examples were $59 per month when reviewed.
- **Address plus receptionist:** Opus widely advertises its virtual-office package at $99 per month, combining an address, mail receipt and phone-answering features.

Those are starting prices, not a promise that every address, add-on or future renewal will cost the same. Opus’s terms say published prices may be promotional and may change with 30 days’ notice.

### Opus may fit if

- You need both a business address and weekday live call answering.
- You want one provider for mail, a business number, call transfer, voicemail and fax delivery.
- You value a large advertised location network more than included coworking access.
- A three-month initial commitment works for your business.

### Think twice if

- You only need mail receipt and do not want to pay for phone services.
- You need a contractually explicit number of receptionist minutes before buying.
- Meeting rooms or day offices must be available at every address.
- You need an address guaranteed for a bank, license, Google Business Profile or registered-agent requirement.

## What the core virtual-office package includes

Opus’s [services page](https://www.opusvirtualoffices.com/services/) presents these features as part of the main virtual-office offer:

- A physical business mailing address and mail receipt.
- Live receptionist answering on weekdays, generally from 8:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. local time, with different published hours for Pacific time locations.
- A company phone and fax number.
- Call transfer to as many as four numbers.
- Two voicemail boxes.
- Voicemail delivered by email as an audio file.
- Fax delivered by email as a PDF.

This is a meaningful package for a small business that would otherwise buy address and answering services separately. It does not mean every related action is included. The terms allow charges for mail content scanning, forwarding, special mail requests and communications usage beyond the allowance of the plan purchased.

## The “unlimited” call-answering question

Opus markets its main offer with “unlimited live call answering.” Its [terms of use](https://www.opusvirtualoffices.com/opus-virtual-offices-terms-of-use/) also state that some service packages contain a specified number of monthly telephone minutes and that usage above a plan threshold may be charged.

These statements can both apply to different plans, but the public wording does not establish the boundary clearly enough for a like-for-like cost comparison. We therefore do not convert “unlimited” into a numerical allowance or assume that every communication action is free. Ask Opus to identify in writing:

- Whether receptionist answering is unlimited for the exact plan selected.
- Whether transfers, outbound legs, messages or after-hours handling consume minutes.
- Which overage rate applies, if any.
- Whether the terms shown at checkout differ from the general website terms.

Until those details are documented for a named plan, our comparison keeps the allowance as **not confirmed** rather than estimating it.

## How mail handling works

Mail service does not begin from the moment you choose an address. Opus requires USPS Form 1583 and two acceptable forms of identification before it can receive mail for a customer. A separate authorization may be required for each location, and additional business names or DBAs can require separate service and fees.

The provider describes professional mail receipt and digital exterior-envelope or package notifications through its Mail-X system. What happens next changes the real cost:

- **Included:** receipt at an approved address is presented as part of the address service.
- **Usage-based:** content scanning, forwarding, postage, shipping and special handling may generate charges.
- **Location-dependent:** pickup procedures, accepted package sizes and other mail policies can vary by facility.

This is why “mail handling included” should not be read as “every mail action included.” Decide whether you will collect mail, forward it on a schedule or scan it frequently, then request the relevant rates.

## Workspace is available, not universal

Opus does not operate every listed address as the same kind of workspace. Its FAQ says conference rooms are available at Premium locations for an hourly fee, while other addresses are mail centers without meeting facilities. The terms also make facility use subject to availability, advance payment and local rules.

Treat workspace as a **paid, location-dependent add-on**, not a standard part of the $99 virtual-office package. If client meetings matter, verify the exact building, room type, hourly price, minimum booking and cancellation policy before choosing the address.

## Paid add-ons and adjacent services

Opus sells more than the core package, but additional services should not be mistaken for standard inclusions.

- **Appointment scheduling:** the provider publishes this as a paid upgrade starting at $100 per month.
- **Supervised transfer and message taking:** each is shown as a paid monthly upgrade on the services page.
- **Outbound caller ID and business texting:** published as separate monthly upgrades.
- **Custom business email:** shown in higher-priced bundle material and onboarding, but not confirmed as included in the base $99 plan.
- **Registered agent and entity formation:** available when separately purchased. The terms describe an annual, non-refundable registered-agent fee but do not publish one nationwide price.
- **Additional business names:** mail service for a DBA is offered as a paid add-on; a separate full virtual-office account may be required when the second name also needs call answering.

These distinctions matter because a buyer seeking scheduling, registered-agent coverage or business email may outgrow the headline package before checkout.

## Contract, cancellation and address limitations

The published terms set a three-month initial term unless another term is stated. The agreement renews until properly ended, and a termination or downgrade request must be submitted through the online account at least 30 days before the end of the existing term. Service ends on the last day of the following complete recurring billing cycle, with no prorated refund.

Setup fees, when applicable, and recurring service fees are paid in advance and described as non-refundable. Opus’s own comparison material displays a $100 setup fee for its virtual-office package, so verify whether that fee appears in the checkout you receive.

The address also has defined limits. Opus says it does not guarantee that a virtual address will be accepted for search listings, licensing, entity formation or financial applications. It cannot be used as a registered office unless registered-agent service is explicitly included. This does not mean the address will fail those uses. It means eligibility depends on the institution, jurisdiction and selected location, and should be confirmed before purchase.

## Advantages and tradeoffs

### Advantages

- A broad core bundle for businesses that need mail and live answering together.
- One account can combine address, phone, fax, transfer and voicemail delivery.
- The provider advertises a large national location network and supports multiple locations for one business.
- Address-only and higher-feature bundles create alternatives to the flagship package.
- Optional scheduling, communications and business-formation services can reduce the number of vendors a buyer manages.

### Tradeoffs

- “Unlimited” marketing is not a substitute for a documented plan allowance and overage rule.
- The three-month initial term and advance cancellation timing reduce short-term flexibility.
- Forwarding, scanning, postage, special mail actions and setup may increase the effective price.
- Meeting facilities are not present at every address and are generally paid separately.
- Address acceptance for banking, licensing, listings or registered-agent use is not guaranteed.
- Local mail and facility policies mean the experience is not perfectly uniform across the network.

## Bottom line

Opus deserves consideration when the buyer’s actual job is “receive business mail and have a person answer calls.” That bundled model is its clearest national advantage. The decision becomes weaker when the buyer needs only one of those services or cannot verify the usage rules, mail costs and facility type attached to the chosen address.

Do not choose Opus from the national headline alone. Choose the service combination first, then verify the exact local offer.
